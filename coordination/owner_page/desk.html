Waiting for you — two findings from the reading measurement

The four reading limits are settled and now in the design (thank you — your words are recorded). The same measurement turned up two things about how reading behaves on this harness. Neither blocks anything; each is yours to settle, and I have not added any check on my own. I recommend an option for each, and "no change" is always available. The two are related (both about reading reliability, both touch part 7), so I bring them together.

Finding 1 — a cut read is silent. When a single text is bigger than the harness's read limit (about 25,000 tokens, roughly 76,000 bytes of dense text), a plain read returns only part of the file, and in this version the result carries no notice that it was cut. A session that did not check would act on part of a text. Two things already guard against this: the single-text limit (60,000) sits below the cut, so no governing text reaches it; and every governing text states its own line count and last section at the top, so a session can tell whether it got the whole file. I have already recorded the fact in design part 7 §2.

  • (a) No further change (recommended). The design now records that a cut is silent, so no one relies on a notice; the 60,000 limit and the line-count header are the guard. Adds nothing.
  • (b) Add a reading practice: each role, after reading a governing text, checks the lines it got against the stated count and re-reads if short. Belt-and-braces; it adds a step to every read. It is self-protection against a harness defect, not a product test — but it is still a step I would not add without your word.

Finding 2 — a headless session cannot see its own fill. The refresh rule now fires at 50% full, which needs a session to read its own fill. In a bare claude -p reader, one session could not see its fill and guessed 70% when it was really 16% — it guessed early, the safe way. Your cloud sessions read their fill a different way (get_session, the route your own PROCESS prompt uses), which does work; the bare-headless case is what cannot. This touches design part 7 §1, which says flatly "the session can read how full its context is."

  • (a) Record the nuance in part 7 §1 (recommended): a session reads its fill via get_session (what the role sessions use), not an in-context display; where a session cannot read its fill it refreshes only at a natural pause (already the rule where a harness shows no fill level). The get_session route is confirmed in the trial. Adds no check.
  • (b) No change — leave part 7 §1 as written. I would not recommend it: the line is now known to be imprecise.

To settle: a marked line, e.g. Decision: finding 1 (a), finding 2 (a) — or correct any part. I record your words and update the design in the same step.
